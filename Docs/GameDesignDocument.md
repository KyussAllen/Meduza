# Meduza

## Elevator Pitch

Meduza is a first-person survival horror game where the player takes the role of a lone fisherman stranded on an abandoned offshore oil rig after a violent storm.

To survive for seven days, the player must explore the decaying structure, fish for food, restore failing systems, and evade a mysterious shapeshifting creature that grows more dangerous with every life it consumes.

---

## Design Pillars

### Isolation

The player should almost always feel alone.

### Vulnerability

The player is never stronger than the monster.

### Curiosity

The player should constantly wonder what happened here.

### Survival

Food, safety, and exploration all matter.

### Atmosphere

The oil rig itself is as dangerous as the creature.

### Core Emotions
I want the player to feel helpless, metagaming aside, there should be no hope for survival, and yet the pursuit of survival should be the only goal. Even if we never add the Jaegers I want the player to feel incapable of fighting the monsters...
...without massive assistance. It should not feel like the weapons provide safety, but merely a distraction or diversion from the players inevtiable or impending doom. Beating the game should not feel like the main goal was accomplished. but almost...
...like an easter egg ending, success should not be guranteed but instead be a distant dream, only accomplishable through tremendous effort and ingenuity.

## Player Experience

The player should feel fundamentally outmatched.

Weapons are tools for survival—not instruments of victory. They buy time, create opportunities, or force the creature to retreat, but they should never make the player feel safe.

Every encounter should reinforce the idea that the monster is the dominant force on the oil rig.

Escaping the rig is not the player's birthright—it is an extraordinary achievement. Completing the game should feel less like defeating an enemy and more like surviving an impossible situation through preparation, ingenuity, and perseverance.
